## Grasp candidates and replay

Before `plan_grasp_batch`, source-container lift includes the full native bounds, retained neighbours and every validated source-shell component. Current CAD scenes contribute all 49 mesh parts; historical rectangular shells contribute five cuboids. Mesh bounds are accepted only for names returned by the complete, current, catalog-hash-validated `published_bin_shell_layers` contract. This does not authorize arbitrary source/neighbor meshes or change the table-support cuboid contract. The shared world-Z offset still places every candidate's full carried-sphere bottom at least 10 mm above the resulting occupied top without changing any scene geometry.

`generate_grasps(snapshot, target_id, arm=..., placement_request=..., planning_settings=...)` requires a frozen object/slot binding and a finite measured support height before inference. When validated `completed_geometry_points_world_m` is available, the demo retains that full measured-plus-prior geometry and appends inference-only samples on its existing convex-hull side surface. The original measured points must remain the completion's leading prefix; completion counts, source label and observation identity must match. Invalid provenance stops before inference. Without completion, the measured cloud is used. The base adapter retains measured-only behavior.

`demo_grasp_input.sample_completed_side_surface` samples the measured XY convex-hull perimeter and the completed geometry's height at at most 2 mm spacing, with a hard limit of 12,000 total submitted object points. Exceeding the limit stops before inference; it does not silently coarsen or drop source points. This spacing describes inference samples, not the 2 cm map voxels. Original measured and completed arrays, attachment geometry and mapping are unchanged. `graspgen_input_sampling` records the scope/source, spacing, source-geometry count, added count and actual total; `cloud` hashes the actual request and counts all synthetic points. The original `completion_provenance` continues to describe the original completion, not newly measured surfaces.

The numerical cloud origin, target identity and placement reference always come from the original measurement. The scene input remains the original registered depth. `cloud` records the exact submitted object array's SHA-256, count and source in the cloud frame; `measured_cloud_reference` separately preserves the original measured digest and count. Synthetic geometry is explicitly labeled, never presented as newly observed depth.

The demo first requests a pool of 256 candidates, increasing to fresh pools of 512 and then 1,000 only after a fully evaluated pool has no executable grasp. These use 3, 6 and 10 service calls respectively. The unchanged official inference settings per call are `num_grasps=200` and `topk_num_grasps=100`; the pool budget is not the number generated by one inference. `raw_candidate_count` records the actual sum returned by the service before trimming to the pool budget, while direction filtering, batched IK and planning counts remain separate. The base adapter retains its legacy default of 200; explicit budgets must be integers in 1–1,000. Empty samples, changed model identity and service errors stop the invocation without a retry or alternate model.

During `plan_grasp`, the next scheduled fresh pool is permitted after either a complete current source-IK result with zero eligible candidates after the accepted-FK checks below, or a complete deterministic planner failure for every eligible candidate in that pool. There are at most three pools including the original pool: 256 + 512 + 1,000 = 1,768 budgeted candidates, through at most 19 service calls. Planning stops as soon as an executable grasp is found. IK-empty and planner-exhausted rounds consume the same budget. All additional inference, IK and trajectory planning share the original `plan_timeout_s` deadline (180 seconds by default) and one planning attempt; it is never restarted per pool. The original initial-generation skill remains outside that planning deadline. Each service call checks cancellation before and after returning, so cancellation or timeout prevents subsequent calls while an already submitted remote call may finish. Unknown or incomplete results do not authorize another pool.

Additional pools reuse the same frozen RGBD, measured frame, labeled completion, target, arm, scene token, settings and bound placement request. A digest of the exact object/scene inference arrays and gripper profile, plus model/frame/version identity, must match before another IK call. No observation, map, Responses or Decisions call occurs. Filtering-empty results, unknown/malformed/stale results, service errors, cancellation, timeout, final-FK rejection, command-validation failures and native hard-check failures stop; they never authorize another pool.

`Algorithms.plan_grasp` publishes `pool_exhaustion` only after traversing its candidate batches. The demo verifies its round, target, arm, generation, scene token, original settings, tool profile and frozen joints against the current evaluation. All eligible original candidate indices must be covered exactly once, with no unplanned candidate. Every failure must be an ordinary planner result with the pinned SDK status `Grasp planning completed.`, no error, explicit boolean phase flags, at least one failed phase and consistent stage ordering. A bare `planning_no_valid_candidate`, partial result, unknown status, or a native hard checker rewriting the status to `CUROBO_TRAJECTORY_COLLISION` cannot authorize another pool. This narrow accepted status does not infer failure from an exception or guess about SDK messages.

After three pools with no eligible source goal, `no_collision_free_source_ik` still reports zero trajectory attempts. If any pool reached trajectory planning and the shared pools are exhausted, `planning_no_valid_candidate` retains the planner failure and `sampling_evidence` with all round outcomes and the actual trajectory-attempt count. An earlier pool failure is not emitted as a terminal failed skill when the next authorized pool succeeds. The frozen `0ce94a9193c54f3bad6a6531abb6a4fc` run used a zero-IK first pool, then two deterministic planner failures in its second pool; this change makes its unused third pool available within the existing deadline. It does not explain the geometric cause of those failures or establish a successful live transfer.

Every candidate replay stage carries the one-based `sampling_round`. Original `index`/`candidate_index`, `source_batch` and `source_index` are preserved; `(sampling_round, index)` identifies a candidate across pools. `sampling_evidence` accompanies the successful plan or exhausted-pool error. Earlier pool artifacts remain available.

Execution uses native joint position servos. Gripper actuator and total joint effort limits come from the project's URDF (8.066 Nm for each driver), including native gravity compensation in the total limit. The frozen closure in experiment `9975165c6b7d4f97bb6a5fc914b314c0` exposed a legacy 200 Nm cap: target contact displaced the wrist by 5.97 degrees despite continuous grasp/lift commands and unchanged arm targets. Restoring the authored cap reduces the same frozen wrist error to 0.0125 degrees. The existing one-degree trajectory-start guard remains; a rejection now records actual/start joints, maximum drift, joint name, generation and feedback time. This dynamics regression does not establish successful lifting or change the 5 mm collision threshold.

The actual tool/mount matrix comes from the selected arm's existing `grasp_targets` profile. A/B preserve the same tool Z and have opposite X/Y axes, as tested against the authored profiles. For each candidate:

1. Save the original world tool transform, then translate its position by `0.01 * R[:, 2]` metres. Rotation, original index, score and source indices stay unchanged.
2. Require `-R[2, 2] >= cos(45 degrees)` within floating-point tolerance.
3. Let `u` be the normalized horizontal vector from own-model robot base to the measured target mean. Require `R[:2, 2] dot u >= 0` within tolerance. This rejects horizontal approach components toward the robot; an exactly vertical approach is accepted.
4. Apply the existing nominal fixed-slot release/approach reach screen before source IK. The measured cloud mean establishes the object offset. Nominal release mount Z is `grasp_z - nominal_object_min_z + bound_floor_z + hard_clearance`; no extra 2 mm is added. `nominal_object_min_z` is the validated completed geometry minimum when present, otherwise the measured minimum. Both nominal wrist distances must be within the own-URDF two-segment reach bound minus a 5 mm inward reserve. Current place approach settings are used. This is conservative quality screening, not an impossibility or collision proof; actual attachment and world checks determine final release Z.

The surviving candidates retain order and score. They enter the existing batched source IK and `plan_grasp_batch` flow. There is no external place-IK batch. The reach bound derives shoulder, wrist/tool offset, Joint3 axis and the two segment lengths from the authored URDF/FK, including a one-micrometre geometry padding; no demonstrated-run pose is hardcoded.

Replay artifacts retain `target_id`, stable indices and three stages: `before_ik`, `after_ik`, and `after_plan_grasp_batch`. Before IK includes original and inserted tool poses, the direction/reach evidence, the registered target cloud and the exact GraspGen request (`scene_points_camera_m`, `object_points_camera_m`, gripper profile), with `T_world_cloud`. `graspgen_input_source` distinguishes `measured` and `height_completed`; `completion_provenance`, `completed_geometry_points_world_m` and `graspgen_input_cloud` retain the prior's source and actual submitted geometry separately from `measured_points_world_m`. The later stages retain exact request/response data and `candidate_indices` mapping local response ordinals to original indices. Arrays remain ndarrays so the experiment recorder can save NPZ references. Failed filtering retains the before-IK replay in error details. Replay does not execute commands.

## Predicted gripper compatibility before execution

After source FK passes the original-nominal tolerance and direction checks, Demo applies `demo_gripper_quality.predict_gripper_compatibility` to the full frozen measured/explicitly completed geometry. It does not use GraspGen-only dense samples. Points, pose, score and indices are unchanged. Ordinary `Algorithms` does not apply this policy.

The model extracts the largest opposing planar surfaces from the authored Link3 collision STLs, using own-model MuJoCo FK and URDF mimics. It verifies parallel normals and a strictly decreasing jaw gap over the driver range at at most 0.01 rad sample spacing. A bounded bisection (at most 24 steps, 1e-8 m width residual) finds one `q_width_rad` where pad gap equals full-cloud width along the closing normal. Out-of-range width explicitly rejects; malformed points, padding or model stop with `grasp_gripper_prediction_invalid`. Completion provenance retains its existing validation/error.

This is a **static rigid-width quality prediction**, not measured closure, cloud centering or continuous closing-sweep proof. At that single angle, every untranslated original point plus the original `uncertainty_m + attachment.margin_m` is checked against all own gripper spheres outside the explicit nine-link hand-contact assembly, including TCP and any unreviewed new link. Sphere radii retain configured collision/self buffers; the world 10 mm threshold is not added again. No fitted attachment spheres are used for this quality filter. Negative minimum gap rejects as `predicted_noncontact_geometry_conflict`.

The same check runs on the final grasp-phase official FK after its original-nominal tolerance/direction checks. Final rejection remains `planning_grasp_endpoint_rejected` and cannot authorize another pool. Source rejection preserves raw IK success and reduces only eligible candidates; zero eligible uses the existing shared three-pool/180-second policy. Actual held feedback, attachment fitting, all hard checks and actual-grasp placement rotation remain unchanged.

Each original source `candidate_filters` row has a nested `predicted_gripper_compatibility`; the final prediction is under `final_grasp_validation`. Evidence includes source/arm/tool, original cloud count/hash, measured count and completion provenance, padding components, width/normal, q bracket/residual, minimum gap and point/link/sphere indices, exemptions and model fingerprint. Existing surrounding round/index bindings remain. Model caching hashes URDF, sphere YAML and pad STLs; these deployment assets and `robot_model()` are immutable within a process. Asset changes require restart, not hot replacement.

Frozen fixtures preserve 1,955 original points (1,361 measured plus 594 labeled completion) and all accepted source-FK goals from 3ce/5be. The earlier Link3-only policy retained 3ce 931 and only 5be 826, rejecting actually selected 508; its archived point-envelope gaps were approximately +2.959, +0.395 and -2.372 mm. The current explicit hand-assembly policy below retains all five 5be source-FK goals; it does not relabel those historical results. Candidate 826's archived lift failed and its fitted attachment spheres overlapped; this screening does not establish an executable transfer. ## Carrying geometry and collision policy

The shared hard world clearance is 10 mm by default and cannot be configured below 10 mm. The wider trajectory-optimizer activation distance remains a cost setting. Project-owned `planning_contracts.py` validates the 10 mm policy while retaining all copied pose/joint/timing validation and vendor-compatible request types. `dual_planning.py` also uses the project contract for the dual planner's internal post-interpolation check. Actual public IK configuration and the hard world checker receive the requested distance; this is not a JSON-only change. No vendor globals are patched.

Actual TSDF/ESDF grids are 20 mm. Attachment `minimum_partition_size_m` is a spatial sphere-fit stopping criterion, default 10 mm, not a voxel resolution. Old stored `voxel_size_m` attachment settings migrate to this name. Carrying spheres permit budgets 1–256; ordinary defaults remain 16, and the demo defaults to 256 only without saved settings. Explicit saved budgets, including 128 and 129–256, are preserved; loading no longer silently downgrades 129–256 to 16. The largest remaining group is split at the midpoint of its first local principal-component projection, so slanted surfaces are not forced into TCP-axis cuts. Every group encloses all its original points with the unchanged measurement uncertainty and attachment margin. Up to 255 splits tighten the approximation without reducing coverage. Picometre rounding stabilizes tied partition and center choices; original coordinates determine every final covering radius.

`placement.carrying_binding` accepts explicitly labeled height-completed geometry only when the original measured point prefix and provenance match. The original measured centroid remains the placement reference. Synthetic points have separate counts and completion provenance; `source_counts_by_sphere` distinguishes measured and height-completion membership of each fitted sphere. Completion is a user-prior approximation, not a sensor measurement or proof of complete object shape.

All attachment points are transformed from world to the measured grasp TCP frame once; the carried link is a fixed identity child of that same authored TCP. The shared `carrying_touch_links(arm)` defines exactly nine owning contact-assembly links: `gripL_Base` plus each L/R `gripL_Link1` through `gripL_Link4` (the corresponding `gripR_` names for B). It is an explicit allowlist, never a prefix wildcard. Only attachment-to-own-assembly pairs are excluded. TCP includes a camera/bracket and remains checked, as do upstream arm links, the opposite hand/arm and unreviewed new links. No sphere or existing ordinary robot-self pair is removed; all hand/object/world geometry and the 10 mm world check remain. Prediction and actual carrying configuration use the same helper.

## Source clearance before grasp trajectory planning

After source IK, the demo derives one shared positive world-Z lift offset for the surviving candidates. `build_scene` publishes `collision_layer_bounds`, bound to its scene token, observation and generation: complete native surface voxel centers expanded by half their 2 cm cell, and the world bounds of published cuboids. `InstanceMapper` reduces all native world centers before its 20,000-point preview sampling, retaining `surface_cell_bounds_world_m`, `surface_cell_bounds_source` and `surface_cell_count`. Safety bounds never fall back to preview extrema. A transferred layer's old bounds become unavailable until measured again; selection retokens still-valid unchanged bounds and updates enabled states. Collision layers themselves remain intact.

The frozen inventory identifies the source container. Its current measured polygon must contain the target reference. Same-parent neighbors contribute only while currently inside that polygon and not in `completed_target_ids`; excluding them from this calculation never disables their map geometry. Missing, stale or nonfinite source bounds stop planning. Unknown parent identities are not invented.

For each IK survivor, fit the same full measured/explicit-completion cloud into the configured attachment envelope (demo default budget 256, explicit saved settings preserved) with unchanged uncertainty and margin, expressed at that candidate's grasp TCP. Let `b_i` be its lowest world sphere surface and `h` the highest retained source-container/neighbor cell or cuboid. The batch offset is `max(configured_lift_offset, max_i(h + hard_clearance - b_i))`. Preserve grasp rotation and the 0.5 m supported offset limit; other lift axes or tool-frame lift are rejected for this demo. There is no additional IK, candidate reranking, map edit or post-execution lift correction.

`planning_settings` retains the original IK identity; `effective_grasp_settings` and `lift_clearance` record the derived batch settings, source IDs/layers, each sphere envelope and completed/outside exclusions. The source exit prediction is not verified holding or a proof of the entire actual carried lift: the existing pregrasp planner still uses the open gripper, and actual post-lift attachment/world validation remains required.

Frozen experiment `9975165c6b7d4f97bb6a5fc914b314c0` lifted physically by 96.53 mm and returned `held` after the effort fix. Yet its padded attachment at the old lift endpoint failed the same 5 mm world check near a remaining pink object's voxel layer; the same arm and measured fingers without attachment passed. This was an isolated frozen diagnostic, not an executed live transfer.

## One fixed release pose

`plan_place` requires verified holding, an applied carrying attachment, current scene identity and the bound region/slot. The measured object reference is placed at the frozen slot center with the actual grasp-success rotation. For local reference `c`, world grasp rotation `R`, and slot center `s`, mount XY is `s - (R c).xy`. The complete attachment footprint must fit the cell and parent region. There is no yaw/XY search or silent alternative goal.
